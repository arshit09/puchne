Developers

Debugging & code review

Paste the trace once. Take the fix that compiles.

Here's the error and the function that throws it. Smallest fix? Then what you'd check if it fails.

  • Grid view — diff the patches by eye
  • Auto-submit off for per-language tweaks
  • Follow up: “now a test that catches it”
Research

Fact-checking

Four takes on one question. Consensus, or a list of things to verify.

What changed here in the last two years? Cite sources, and say where you're unsure.

  • Add a search-grounded tool to the mix
  • Check the citations — models invent them
  • Follow up: “least confident part?”
Writing

Four drafts, one screen

Keep the best sentence from each.

Half as long, twice as direct. Keep my voice; no adjectives I didn't use.

  • Select on the page → Ctrl+Shift+S
  • Follow up: “one sentence shorter”
Students

Cross-check before you trust it

Three explanations that agree are probably sound. One that differs sends you to the textbook.

Explain this in plain language with one worked example. Then what people get wrong.

  • Follow up: “quiz me, one question at a time”
  • Check your institution's rules on AI first
Translation

Tone, not just words

Literal translation is solved. Register isn't.

Into Gujarati, for a formal client email. Which phrases did you change to stay polite?

  • Ask each to flag its judgement calls
  • Follow up: “warmer, less formal”
Product

Names & positioning

Four models in parallel spread wider than one model four times.

Ten names. One line on what each implies, one risk. No two-noun compounds.

  • Follow up: “hardest to trademark, and why?”
Documents

Contracts, papers, logs

Long and unfriendly. Let them argue about what the clause means.

What does this oblige me to do? Flag anything one-sided and quote the wording.

  • Right-click → Ask Puchne, Show Prompt on
  • Reading help, not legal or medical advice
Learning

Three teachers, three analogies

Keep the metaphor that clicks. The rest cost nothing.

Explain like I'm 12 — one analogy, no jargon. Then where the analogy breaks.

  • Follow up: “now at degree level”
